# cfo notify

`cfo notify <id> --done --pr <url> \| --blocked "<question>" \| --failed "<reason>" \| --working "<what>" \| --waiting-on <task-id\|overlord\|ci\|deploy\|memory> "<why>" [--lavish <html-file>] [--link <https-url>] [--run <command-file>] [--follows <question-id> \| --separate] [--message-file <file>]`

A goblin reports its outcome (PR URL, blocked question, or failure reason) straight into the wake queue, waking the CFO with the real payload instead of the watcher guessing from pane text.
A `--done` or `--failed` report also names, in its line and its wake, what the goblin started that still runs beside the report's own tool call, a machine service aside, as the supervisor's start watch places it, and tells the goblin to end what it no longer needs by its pid.
The tool call's own is never named: the report and what runs under it, every process attached to the console of the shell it was sent from, that console's host, and every process those run under.
So neither that shell, nor the console host Windows starts beside its hidden console, nor a program of the report's pipeline such as `tail` is named, though Git Bash starts such a program, or a `timeout` the report runs under, by replacing its own Windows process, which leaves it no parent.
A program the tool call started with a console of its own is named, and one it started in the background on its own console is left to the start watch's `left_running` wake, which names it once the goblin has rested a minute with it still running.
Where a harness runs its tool calls in its terminal's own console, which everything the goblin started there shares, the console counts for nothing, and only the report, what runs under it and what it runs under are left out.
What a report would name it reads again two seconds later, and it names only what still runs then by its pid and its start, so a tool call run beside the report that ends meanwhile is not named. **`--done` is per pull request, not per task: it requires `--pr` and records the literal line `done: PR <url>`, and a task can ship several under one id, which is the ordinary shape for a landing task.** So a terminal verb in a status log establishes that a pull request finished and nothing whatever about whether the goblin is still working; anything that reads it as "the goblin is gone" is wrong, and the more destructive the thing it decides, the more wrong.
Live evidence answers that question: a pane holding an agent working in the goblin's worktree.
A question goes out as `cfo notify <id> --blocked "<one short question> options: <answer> (Recommended) \| <answer>"`: the question in one short sentence, every detail on a line of its own that starts with `- ` and comes before `options:`, and the choices after `options:` on its line, each the answer itself as a short phrase, such as `Fix it next` or `Keep 300 s`.
`cfo drain` shows it as a decision with those choices listed.
A detail on a line after the choices is recorded before them, so it never joins a choice.
A question that writes `options:` more than once, has fewer than two choices after it on its line, or offers a choice that is only a letter or number is refused before anything is recorded, with how to ask.
The same question sent again while it still waits unanswered is the same decision: the goblin is told, and the CFO is not woken again.
`--working` and `--waiting-on` are status for the board: they wake nobody and replace a stale blocked or failed reading, except that waiting on the Overlord wakes the CFO and puts an item in the Command Center, which a later `--blocked` question does not replace (the goblin waits on both, so it never files the wait again); a wait on another task clears itself when that task reports done, and the CFO releases any wait with `cfo notify <id> --working "<what>"`.
Since they wake nobody, a `--working` report or a wait on anything but the Overlord that asks a question with choices (choices after `options:` in any case, a choice marked (Recommended), or choices split by `|` after a question mark) is refused before anything is recorded and told to go as `--blocked`, so the question reaches the CFO as a decision and the board reads the goblin as blocked.
A wait on the Overlord while a step card the goblin filed itself is open is that card, as a wait with `--run` is its run card: no second item opens, the notify names the card and tells the goblin to wait on it in the background with `cfo auth request --wait-on <card> --task <id>`, which ends when the card closes, and the board reads the goblin as waiting until it reports again, as for a run card.
Done on the card, or its inputs arriving, also closes a wait the goblin opened while it was open.
A wait on the Overlord that needs his answer on a Scrawl page names it with `--lavish <html-file>`: the notify opens the page without a browser and refuses, recording nothing, when it cannot; the page's link goes into the wait's line and onto its Command Center item, and the supervisor polls the page, so his feedback there reaches the CFO as a `review` wake with the whole reply saved under `state/reviews/feedback/`, and the item closes.
While AFK mode is on the same wait is instead that goblin's question to the CFO, which answers it with `cfo answer` in his place ([AFK mode](../contract/afk.md#afk-mode)).
A goblin never runs `lavish-axi poll` itself: that poll takes his feedback where only the goblin sees it.
`--waiting-on overlord` is only for a wait on the Overlord personally: his sign-in, his click, his page.
Such a wait is a card in his Command Center only when it names what he acts on: `--lavish`, `--link`, `--run`, a step card or file the goblin filed, or a decision it `--follows`; one that names none wakes you with ` (no card: it names nothing he acts on)` on its line and opens no card, so put the step to him yourself as a card that acts (`cfo run-request`, `cfo question`) or answer the goblin.
Lead such a wait with one sentence; values he must enter somewhere go in a Markdown table on the lines after it, each value in backticks so his card copies it with one click (the notify help shows the form), and the one place he goes is `--link <https-url>`, the only link his card opens.
A command he must run himself, such as a sign-in, goes in a `.ps1` or `.sh` file named with `--run <file>` on the wait or the question: his Command Center shows it on a run card named for the goblin and runs it with one click in a terminal on his card, where he types into it, and the goblin is told how it ended.
A `--blocked` question with choices, or a `--run` command, carries a message he sends himself, such as an email, with `--message-file <file>` in the form `cfo auth request` takes: the question's card shows it when it offers choices, the run card otherwise, and a report no card shows, such as a helper's question to its parent, refuses it before anything is recorded.
A choice the CFO can make, such as whether a goblin starts something now or after a reset, is an actual question and uses `--blocked` with options, so it reaches the CFO rather than sitting in the Overlord's Command Center as a wait.
While a decision for the goblin is open, its own `--blocked` question with choices or your `cfo question --for <its id>`, a `--waiting-on overlord` declares what it is: `--follows <question-id>` when the wait is that decision, which is then hidden while the decision is open and closed by its answer, so he sees one card, or `--separate` when it is a card of its own, as a wait with `--lavish`, `--link` or `--run` always is.
A wait that declares neither is refused before anything is recorded, naming each open decision by its question ID and first line, and so is a `--follows` that names no open decision; nothing guesses which a wait is, since a need he never sees stalls the goblin and an extra card does not.
With no decision open a wait needs neither flag.
