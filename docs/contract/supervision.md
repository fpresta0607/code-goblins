## Supervision

- `cfo fleet-view` is your fleet truth; judge work from it, never from guessing.
  It lists goblins in the Supreme Overlord's attention order, which he sets by dragging In progress on the board (`state/attention.json`): check and unblock from the top.
- The Claude Code hooks (`cfo hook turnend-guard`, `cfo hook stop-autoarm`) refuse to let a turn end blind while goblins are in flight, keep watching while queued work could start with no goblin in flight, and reopen your turn when it ends with no goblin at work while work waits and memory is free ([The fleet never idles](dispatch.md#the-fleet-never-idles)).
  Both fire at a turn's end: `stop-autoarm` shows it is arming before anything slow, and `turnend-guard` waits for a hook that is arming, up to ten seconds, before it calls the turn blind, so a slow start on a loaded machine is not read as supervision being off.
  While `cfo serve` holds the watcher, `stop-autoarm` still rewakes you once for each new wake record.
  It counts serve as the watcher until serve's heartbeat is older than the stall window (15 minutes, `CFO_WATCHER_STALL`), since a slow reconcile cycle can hold serve's heartbeat past five minutes and nothing can take the watcher from a live serve; past that it reports supervision down naming serve's pid and to restart cfo serve.
  When its eight-hour wait ends with nothing queued, it rewakes you to end a turn, which re-arms it, so an idle CFO is never left with no hook watching.
  Every monitor scan and orphan sweep, in `cfo serve` and in `cfo watch`, is cut off after three minutes, and the gate status call it makes after thirty seconds, so a subprocess that never answers cannot stop the heartbeat while the watcher keeps its lock, which would leave supervision off with nothing able to take it over (`internal/watch/reconcile_budget_test.go`).
- A missing or stale endpoint means inspect with `cfo peek`, then steer or relaunch - never kill work.
- **`cfo drain` is how you learn a goblin finished.
  Not `cfo peek`.** Goblins already push terminal outcomes into the wake queue with `cfo notify <id> --done --pr <url> | --blocked "<question>" | --failed "<reason>"`.
  On 2026-08-19 the CFO polled panes roughly eighty times to infer state that was sitting unread in the queue the whole time - two goblins had already filed correct `--done` notifies with their PR URLs.
  Those eighty sweeps produced two real interventions and consumed most of a context window.
  Drain first, always; `peek` is for reading a goblin's reasoning once the queue has told you it needs you.
- **Every drain ends with a `WAKE_ACK_REQUIRED` line.
  Run it.** An unacked record resurfaces on the next drain and makes a handled goblin look unhandled.
  That line is refused while unanswered `--blocked`/`--failed` notifies sit at or below its sequence: drain lists every waiting goblin and retires nothing.
  The refusal is the protection working, not an error - a record acked unread is a record nobody will ever read.
  Answer each listed goblin with `cfo send <id> "..."`, then re-run the same command with `--ack-blocking`.
  That flag is range-scoped, not per-record: it retires **every** question at or below the sequence, and the refusal listing is the whole set it will retire.
  The ack floor only moves forward, so a later question cannot be retired while an earlier one is kept - to hold one open, handle it first or ack a range that stops below its sequence.
- **An unanswered decision keeps asking, and `cfo drain` prints it as a decision.** A goblin waiting on you is rendered with its own block: which goblin, how long it has been waiting, the question, and the options it offered.
  On 2026-09-18 two goblins waited 8h 47m on a CFO decision while the watcher cycled the whole time, because the monitor treated a wake it had emitted once as a wake that had been answered.
  It no longer does: while the wake ledger holds an unanswered question for a goblin - its own `--blocked`/`--failed` notify, the watcher's decision signal for a goblin that filed none, or the monitor's own awaiting-answer stall for a goblin that ended its turn at its prompt having filed nothing at all - the monitor re-asks on a widening interval capped at one hour, and the heartbeat is barred from backing off.
  The question is whether you owe the goblin an answer, never whether it filed a notify: a goblin that just stops at its prompt asks nothing, and it was that class that went quiet.
  An informational `--done` notify is not a question and never re-asks, and while one sits unacknowledged it also suppresses that goblin's turn-ended wake - it reported an outcome nobody has read yet, so it is finished rather than waiting.
  Acking it is what ends the suppression, so a goblin you acked and then steered back to work re-asks normally the next time it stops at its prompt; only the queue decides this, never the `done:` line sitting in its status file from hours ago.
  Acking the record is the only thing that stops it - answering the goblin without acking leaves the question outstanding as far as the fleet can tell.
- **Poll only with a reason** - a suspected wedge, a CI result you are gating a merge on, or a goblin silent well past when it should have notified. "Checking in" is not a reason.
- **When you do need the roster, enumerate it once - the Stop hook is not the roster.** The hook fires when a goblin's turn ENDS, so a goblin inside a long turn is invisible to it: on 2026-08-19 one goblin spent 1h 1m in one turn holding an escalation nobody answered, and another spent 1h 13m unseen.
  A goblin that has not notified and is not in the hook's list still exists.
- **The gate daemon is the authoritative "needs a decision" signal, not the pane.** `no-mistakes axi status` in a goblin's worktree reports `awaiting_agent` and an awaiting-findings count while the goblin is still mid-turn; pane text does not.
- **A stale wake means the goblin's own evidence stopped, not that its turn is old.** Before the monitor raises `busy_turn_over_age`, `awaiting_answer` or `unchanged_idle`, it reads the goblin's family tree, the reader the board's tree reads through: the goblin's harness transcript (Claude, Codex and Pi: the session Herdr reports, the session the board recorded for its generation, or for Claude Code the session its own running process records, never the newest file in its folder), the transcripts and activity of the sub-agents, background shells and monitors under it, and the processes its harness started after launching (tool commands, background jobs, monitor loops; its MCP servers start with it and never count), with the runs it left detached in the background, such as a test suite a script started, that carry its terminal's mark or work in or run a program from its task's folders, so a goblin counts as working while any child works.
  A server, a browser or a machine service such as the no-mistakes daemon never counts as its work.
  A turn whose transcript was written, or whose own processes used at least 5% of a processor, within the busy budget (one hour) is progressing however old it is, and that processor use is judged reading by reading over spans of at least a minute, while a lack of it is concluded only after a full stall interval (ten minutes) of readings; a goblin that ended its turn with its own job still running and moving is waiting on that job, reads busy, and is asked about only when the job is gone or has shown no progress for the budget, when the wake names what is still running.
  Once raised, the same goblin is raised again only after its evidence has moved and stopped again, and the same `busy_turn_over_age` wake (the same gate step, no progress, or unreadable progress) no sooner than a whole busy budget after the last, so a scan that loses sight of a wedge for a moment does not raise it again as new.
  Evidence that cannot be read is no evidence, but what could be read still counts: when Herdr's process-info fails, a transcript written, or processor use read, within the budget holds the wake, and only when none of it has moved for the budget does the wake fire, saying the evidence was unreadable.
- **A native terminal keeps no counters, so between turns its screen and transcript are its liveness.** Herdr counts a pane's changes and a native terminal does not, so before an `unchanged_idle` wake, output written to a native goblin's screen since a scan that also found it between turns, or its transcript written within the stall window, counts as the movement Herdr's counters would show.
  A clock ticking in a row the harness redraws by itself is not output.
- **A pane showing work running holds a stale wake while the goblin's evidence moves.** A row among a pane's last ones that shows a tool, a turn or a background job running, whichever harness drew it (the `esc to interrupt` hint, Codex's `Working (` row, Claude Code's spinner, its `Running…` under a tool and the background shells and monitors its footer counts, pi's `Working` rule), holds back `busy_turn_over_age`, `awaiting_answer` and `unchanged_idle`, but not for ever: a turn that ended is held only while its transcript or its own processes showed progress within the busy budget of the turn ending, and a working turn until neither has for twice the budget, and the wake then names the pane row still showing work and how long nothing moved; a gate step active past the budget still wakes at once, because that is the gate's own evidence.
  A native screen read that fails keeps the last reading and is read again on the next scan, and only a second failure in a row wakes as `endpoint_unknown`; an unknown or missing endpoint wakes once as it enters that state, and the heartbeat carries it after that.
  Each wake held back is counted once per episode beside the wakes raised, in `state/monitor/tally.json`, and `cfo doctor` prints the counts.
- **An idle goblin wakes you in three minutes, whatever its harness.** A live goblin at its prompt with nothing running on its pane, no question open, no report, and no transcript write or processor use by its own processes for three minutes wakes you once as `goblin_idle`, with the end of its screen and what to do next.
  It is read from what the supervisor sees itself (the screen, the status log, the wake queue, the harness's transcript and processes), so a Codex or pi goblin running without its hooks wakes the same as a Claude Code one, and a process a goblin left idle no longer holds it quiet for the busy budget.
  A goblin that reported done, waits on something with `--waiting-on`, or has a question open is not idle with work left and never wakes this way.
  Until you ack it, it is an answer owed and re-asked like `awaiting_answer`; the same goblin wakes as idle at most once every fifteen minutes.
- **What the fleet starts is watched.** Every five seconds `cfo serve` places what each running goblin and your own terminal started, as the family tree places a goblin's processes: what its harness started after launching, with everything under it, and the trees it left detached, by its terminal's mark or its task's folders.
  It ends nothing: ending stays with the pause, the stop, the cleanup, the relaunch and the janitor's sweep.
  It wakes you once, as a `check` wake keyed by the goblin, or `cfo` for your terminal, naming the pid and the command line with any secret left out, for each of three things.
  `window_shown:` a process of theirs shows on the Overlord's screen, by a window of its own or by a console that shows in one, a classic console window or a tab of Windows Terminal, this machine's default terminal, such as a browser a test left headed, a terminal a test opened or a browser a goblin opened.
  A console window that a goblin's process shows, a classic console window or the one Windows Terminal shows a console in, the supervisor also hides at the reading that sees it, once, without ending anything, and the wake says it hid it and tells you to have the goblin start its heavy runs with `cfo gate turn --detach`.
  Windows Terminal minimizes the window it showed the console in, the one way to take it off his screen from outside the process.
  The console of a program the Overlord let run as an administrator is out of the supervisor's reach, since Windows lets no process attach to the console of one that runs above it: it stays, and the wake says it shows.
  A window of your own terminal's is left for you.
  `start_loop:` one owner's same command line was seen new at ten readings within two minutes, once for each command line in each spawn generation: a dozen short commands of one tool call running at one reading are one reading, not a loop.
  Shells, waits, console hosts, pipeline filters such as `grep` and `base64`, the harness's own tool calls and hooks, machine services, and what a test run, a build or any run in the machine's heavy line starts, a process the run left without its parent too, start no loop, since a poll, a script's pipelines, a loop over repositories, a goblin's own turns and a test suite within its own time limit start them again and again.
  `left_running:` a goblin whose last report is done or failed rests at its prompt, as the janitor reads a goblin at rest, waiting on no shell, monitor, sub-agent or heavy-line run of its own that has shown something new within the busy budget, where a run still waiting in the line shows the line moving: the turn passing to the next run, or its own place taken, with something it started still running a minute later, once for each report, naming each tree with the programs under it.
  The Overlord's own programs, which no terminal of the fleet started, are never placed and never told, and nor is a browser the shell started to open a link, such as a sign-in's, with everything it starts: it is his browser from then on, though it started in a terminal's tree.
  Nor is the supervisor, `cfo serve`, with everything it runs, such as its own gate-status reads: it is the fleet's own service, though an update in a terminal started it and its console host carries that terminal's mark.
  A browser a goblin starts itself, headless or headed, is still the goblin's.
  Read every five seconds, a loop of processes that each live under a second with pauses between can be missed, and the loop itself still shows on the board and in the goblin's done report.
  On 2026-10-11, with seven terminals at work, a dry run of the watch on the live machine took 58 ms a reading at the median and 188 ms at the 90th percentile, 0.9 percent of one processor, and would have woken nobody in ten minutes.
  The thresholds come from ten minutes of such readings, in which, test runs and builds aside, no command line but a shell's, a wait's or a console host's was seen as a new process more than five times in any two minutes.
- **A wake comes once, when something is new.** A goblin's `--done` for one pull request head wakes you once however often it reports it, and the goblin is told you were not woken again.
  A pause, resume or stop you typed sends no notice when it ends, since its outcome is on your screen; one the board, the scheduler or the allowance floor started still tells you, and a pause or stop that ran out of time with the goblin's host gone, as asked, raises no `endpoint_missing`.
  Nor does a goblin whose terminal `cfo cleanup` closed: the cleanup retires the task's record only seconds after it closes the terminal, so a scan that reads the terminal while the cleanup holds the task, or after it retired it, keeps and raises nothing of it.
  A goblin whose `cfo spawn` still holds its turn to start its host, or whose `cfo switch` is relaunching it, is launching, not missing, and the launch budget runs from its host's start.
  A goblin waiting on a sub-agent, background shell or monitor it started, shown by Claude Code's `Waiting for N background` row or its footer's count, or still open in its transcript whatever processes it runs, is not idle until that wait has shown no progress for the busy budget.
  Nor is one whose run waits in or holds the machine's heavy line, since `cfo gate turn` tells it how the run ended.
- **A goblin that stops asking in prose wakes you with its question.** When a goblin at its prompt ended its last reply asking you something or offering you a choice instead of filing `cfo notify --blocked` (a question mark, "unless you want", "should I", "Your answer decides", options for you to pick), the wake that would say its turn ended or it sat idle is `goblin_asks` instead: it names the goblin, says to answer with `cfo send <id> "<answer>"` (`cfo answer` takes only a notify's question) and ends by quoting the sentences that ask.
  The reply is read from the goblin's own Claude Code conversation, Codex rollout or pi session, the one its family tree reads (the session Herdr names, else the one the board recorded for its generation, else the one its running Claude Code process records for itself, or for Codex and pi the rollout or session of its worktree written last), never merely the newest in its folder for Claude Code; a reply pi cut off at its output cap, failed or aborted is no reply, and its thinking never is; only a reply's last two paragraphs are read, and one that only reports, waits on CI or says it is done asks nothing and wakes as before.
  A goblin whose own background command or monitor still moves is not at a stop and is not asked about.
  The board shows the goblin waiting on the CFO with the question, as it shows a blocked notify, until the goblin reports again or you ack the wake, and until you ack it, it is an answer owed and re-asked; it is one wake until the goblin works again, and its next ask is a wake of its own.
- **After a restart or sign-out the fleet comes back by itself.**
  The first `cfo serve` of a Windows sign-in brings back the registered CFO, on its own conversation where that is small enough and on a new one where not, told why it is back, then each goblin that was working and had not finished, in its own session, one at a time as memory allows, and tells each in one line that the machine restarted; while anything waits to come back, nothing else starts by itself and `memory_ready` waits.
  The scheduler's queued starts and paused goblins' automatic resumes wait; a Start or Resume the Overlord presses on the board, or a `cfo spawn` you choose, is still allowed.
- **Restart PC frees the kernel's pools.**
  The supervisor's minute memory reading keeps a record of the kernel's paged and nonpaged pools for each sign-in in `state/kernel-pools.json`, with what each pool tag holds, so a pool that grows for days names the tag behind it, and the board's **Kernel pools** meter shows both beside memory.
  Each sample also counts the processes the PC started and the tokens the kernel holds, and the record names what the board itself started by program and caller: every process start leaves the kernel a token, about two in five of them for good on 2026-10-19, so what the fleet starts is what fills the pool.
  So the board answers the reads it repeats every minute, each goblin's heads, branches, refs, reflog, worktrees and status and its gate run in the gate's database, from what the read last returned while the files it reads are unchanged, and a refresh over work nobody changed starts no git and no sqlite3; and every fleet program runs Git for Windows' `mingw64\bin\git.exe` rather than the `cmd\git.exe` launcher on PATH, one process for a git command rather than two.
  Past the 7.5 GB line measured on the Overlord's PC, where free memory can no longer hold more than one goblin at a time, the CFO panel's header offers him **Restart PC** on the board on this PC alone, proven his as AFK mode's on is.
  His press wakes you first with a `memory` wake keyed `restart`: start, resume and merge nothing, and end your turn.
  The supervisor then pauses every working goblin until the PC has restarted, each writing its handoff first, ends the runs in the heavy line, waits up to three minutes for your turn to end, wakes you with what it did and restarts Windows; after he signs in the comeback brings you back and the goblins paused for it resume by themselves.
  A goblin that does not pause stops the restart short, and you are told why: tell him, since only he presses it again ([the native board guide](../native-board.md)).
  Do not bring those goblins back by hand: a goblin that cannot come back stays stopped with the reason on its card and wakes you once as a `check` wake naming `cfo switch <id> --harness <its harness>`, which brings it back once the cause is fixed.
- **Your terminal ending unasked brings you back by itself.**
  When your terminal ends in this sign-in while nobody asked it to stop, as when your harness crashes or ends itself after an update, `cfo serve` brings you back within about ten seconds (8 to 12 in a live proof), on the harness you ran, as Restart the CFO would: on your conversation where it is small enough, and on a new one where its context is past the 400K tokens you compact within, Claude Code wrote no transcript for it, or it does not resume.
  Your first instruction then says you are the CFO started again, why, and on which conversation, and to read the digest, drain your wakes and carry on with the fleet; nobody types it.
  A Claude Code CFO back on its own conversation follows its freshly composed hook's `READ THIS NEXT`, rereads changed authority and skips unchanged source bytes only if it actually read and still holds them.
  Every start that brings back a CFO that ran in the home before carries such an instruction: this one, the comeback after a restart, the board's Reopen, Restart the CFO and its Update, and `goblins`; a home's first CFO waits for the Overlord as before.
  An end somebody asked for stays: a terminal closed through its host, as Restart, a harness switch and the uninstall close it, and a harness that exited with code 0, as its own exit does.
  Each end is acted on once, from what the terminal's host recorded in `state\hosts\cfo.end` (its program, exit code, and whether it was closed), and the supervisor's own record in `state\cfo-back.json`.
  A CFO that keeps ending soon after it started is not restarted in a loop: after its third end in a row, each of a CFO that ran under ten minutes, with no more than ten minutes between one end and the next start, it stays stopped, with one `check` wake keyed `cfo` for the next CFO and one Command Center item for the Overlord whose one action is Reopen the CFO; a comeback that fails without a newer end leaves it stopped the same way, a CFO it started that ended during startup is one more end in the row, and a CFO that runs again takes the item back.
  When that wake reaches you, find why your terminal ends, from your last transcript and that end record, before it ends again.
  On 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-17 the CFO's Claude Code ended with exit code 1 right after a turn ended, in conversations of 634 MB and 404 MB, and stayed closed until the Overlord reopened it and typed into it, the first time for 16 hours.
- **Memory coming back wakes you when work waits for it.** `cfo serve` reads the memory meter once a minute, and once available memory and commit headroom both read at or above the 5 GB next-start mark on two readings in a row, while a queued task could start or a live goblin's latest report is `waiting on memory`, it wakes you once as `memory_ready` with the numbers and the next step: the next startable task in queue order, and the goblins to release.
  It wakes again only after a reading falls under the 4 GB floor and crosses back, and never twice within fifteen minutes.
- **CI finishing wakes you.** `cfo serve` asks GitHub through the authenticated `gh` every two minutes, one PR list, one main-run list and one batched comparison of any open PRs per watched repository: the repositories live goblins work in, kept for six hours after the last one leaves.
  When every check on a live goblin's open pull request (the one it recorded with `cfo pr check`, reported `done`, or opened from a branch its worktree has or had checked out) has concluded, it wakes you once as `ci_finished`, keyed by the goblin, with the result and the failing checks; a rerun that finishes with a new result wakes again.
  In a repository whose checkout has a workflow its pull requests trigger, it also waits for a workflow's check: a head none reports for within ten minutes of the rest wakes you saying no workflow ran, never that all its checks passed, and a pull request that conflicts with its base raises no `ci_finished` at all, since GitHub runs none of its workflows; its `pr_health` conflict wake says so.
  When the newest push run of a workflow on a repository's default branch finishes red, it wakes you once for that run, keyed `main:<repository>`, naming the workflow, the failing jobs and the run id.
  Each of these wakes, and the one for a paused goblin's awaited run, also names the tests that failed once and passed on their second try in the runs it reports, as their workflow named them in warnings titled Failed once ([What CI runs](delivery.md#what-ci-runs)): such a test leaves its run green, so the wake is where you learn of it, and a test named there fails by chance and wants a fix before it fails twice.
  They are read in one more call as the wake is raised, never on a poll that raises none, and a read that fails is said in the wake rather than passed over as none.
  Runs are judged newest first by their ids, and GitHub sometimes answers with an older page of them, so a listing whose newest run is older than one a poll already read for that repository, before or since it was last watched, is passed over until the next poll.
  One pull request wakes at most once every five minutes, and one workflow on one repository's default branch wakes at most once every five minutes, so a push that turns two workflows red raises two wakes, each naming its own workflow and run; a result its gap holds waits for the gap.
  A repository with no `origin` remote is local, and one whose `origin` is a path, a `file://` URL or a host GitHub does not serve is hosted elsewhere: the fleet reads pull requests, tickets, trains and task evidence on github.com only, so all of them are unwatched - no `gh` call, no error, no wake, and never `gh`'s sign-in advice.
  The checkout is read before its `origin`, and a checkout that is missing, is no Git checkout, is only a folder inside another repository, or that git refuses to read, and an `origin` naming a GitHub host but no repository, keep one line in the supervisor's error and wake you as `ci_unreadable` with the path or setting that repairs them.
  One that has a `github.com` `origin` and still cannot be read (`gh` is signed out, a `gh` call fails, or its default branch cannot be found) keeps one line in the supervisor's error on the board until a poll reads it again, and once the same failure has been met on three polls in a row it wakes you once as `ci_unreadable`, keyed `repo:<repository>`, with the error and the next step: sign `gh` in with `gh auth login`, point `origin` at GitHub, or set `origin`'s default branch with `git remote set-head origin -a`.
  No CI wake comes from that repository until it reads again.
  It wakes again only for a different failure met on three polls in a row or for one that cleared and came back, never twice within five minutes for one repository; a failure clearing raises no wake.
  The failure is compared by its exact text, so one whose text differs on every poll keeps its line on the board and does not wake, and one whose text changes, as when a goblin starts in a repository `gh` cannot read, wakes once more.
- **PR health wakes you on the same poll.** The fleet watches its own pull requests: a goblin's PR wherever it is, and every open PR, a teammate's too, in a watched repository the fleet owns, even with no live goblin there.
  A repository is the fleet's when its GitHub owner, read from the PR's own address and never from a remote's name, is the account `gh` works as or an organization `config/fleet.json`'s `github_owners` names; another owner's PRs, such as the upstream's in a checkout of a fork whose `origin` is the upstream, raise nothing.
  Each watched PR is checked for a conflict with its base and for falling behind the repository's current default branch.
  A PR is a live goblin's when the goblin recorded it or reported it `done`, when its head is the branch the goblin's worktree has checked out, or when the account `gh` works as opened it from a branch one of the goblin's worktrees had checked out before.
  A goblin's worktrees are its worktree, its extras, and every worktree of its project that git registers inside its worktree or its scratch folder, such as one it adds there for a second pull request.
Git keeps every checkout in the worktree's own reflog, so a goblin that goes on to its second branch still owns the PR of its first, and a supervisor or a goblin that restarts reads the same answer again: nothing about whose a PR is lives in the supervisor's memory.
  A branch the goblin only looked at makes nothing its own, since a PR another account opened is a goblin's only when the goblin said so or has its branch checked out.
  One rule decides a head that is behind, for every PR a live goblin holds: it asks for nothing until the PR is next to land with no merge train to test it on current main, because bringing main in replaces the head and the run on the head it replaced paid for nothing (in PrecisionDocs about $27 a month, at 3.3 runs per merge).
  A PR the account `gh` works as opened in this repository is never next to land, since a train takes it and tests it on the main it lands on: the supervisor starts one for two or more, and for one alone once it waited 10 minutes.
  Any other goblin's PR is next once its goblin reported it `done` and its checks passed.
  That holds whichever of its branches the PR is on, and a held PR is never next to land, since no train takes it.
  A behind head whose checks failed, have not run or still run asks for nothing: a failure is `ci_finished`'s to tell, and is no reason to bring main in.
  A conflict always wakes you, and so does a behind head no live goblin holds, a teammate's and the fleet's own from a branch no goblin holds, once its checks are no longer running, for three hours at most.
  A draft that is only behind raises nothing, since it is not yet the head its author means to merge.
  One GraphQL request compares the watched head SHAs, fork heads included, without conditional ETags for POST reads.
  A poll raises at most one `pr_health` wake of kind `pr` per repository, keyed `health:<owner>/<name>`, naming each PR whose head fell into a condition you were not woken for.
  A goblin's PR names the goblin and the safe update: merge the default branch in with a merge commit, regenerate generated files rather than hand-merging them, one CI run and never force-push.
  A teammate's PR is one another account than the one `gh` works as opened: its wake names the author and link and reports only, and the fleet never pushes to that PR.
  A PR that account opened itself from a branch no live goblin holds, the Overlord's own or a finished task's, is called neither a goblin's nor a teammate's and is reported the same way, and while the account cannot be read the wake says only that no live goblin holds the PR.
  Each condition wakes once per head across restarts and long-lived PRs, and a PR whose head and condition stay as they were is never named again; conflicts take priority, a behind head waits up to ten minutes for GitHub to work out whether it conflicts, a held wake waits out five minutes after the repository's last one, and failed or absent checks do not hold a behind wake.
  Null or invalid comparisons and a listing at the 100-PR limit are explicit unread evidence, never proof that everything is current.
- **The supervisor's own errors wake you once.** Each new line of what the supervisor meets, such as a store it cannot save or a setting it cannot read, wakes you once as a `check` wake keyed `supervisor`, starting `supervisor_error:`.
  A line that stays, or comes back with each cycle, does not wake again until it has gone unmet for an hour, and new lines wait out five minutes after the last such wake rather than being dropped.
  A read the supervisor makes on a timer that fails, such as one that times out under the fleet's load, is read again on the next pass and reaches you only once it failed on three passes in a row: the overlap read, an awaited run, a finished task's pull request, a merge train's step, its start, the repository it runs on and the account it reads, the CI listings behind `ci_unreadable`, a repository's origin, a goblin's progress, a paused goblin's condition, the pull request a queued row waits on, the history's scan of a repository's merges and of the pull request heads its origin publishes, and the sweep's read of a review page no poller watches.
  On 2026-10-08 such reads each woke you on one timeout and took about 600 ms minutes later.
  Unreadable CI is left to `ci_unreadable` and `pr_unread`.
  They keep a line on the board and wake you as `pr_unread` (kind `pr`, keyed `repo:<repository>`) once per head whose own comparison failed, conflicting or not, and once for the listing limit until it clears, with the next step; recurring poll errors wake no more, they never raise `ci_unreadable`, and readable PR health and CI wakes continue.
  A comparison request that fails as a whole (refusal, timeout, server error, no comparisons returned) keeps only its board line and wakes for no head; a refusal reaches you as `ci_unreadable`.
  A 403, 429 or exhausted allowance pauses all GitHub calls in this poll for that repository across restarts until the later of its usable Retry-After and reset time, or an hour when neither is usable; other repositories continue.
- **A terminal on the system conhost wakes you once.** A native terminal runs on the console host cfo.exe embeds, Microsoft's OpenConsole, and when its host cannot write or load it the terminal runs on Windows' inbox conhost, which can strand a typed key or crash, and you are woken once per home as a `check` wake keyed `conpty`, starting `console_host:` with the reason ([docs/native-board.md](../native-board.md)).
- **New teammate overlaps wake you on the same poll.** One shared overlap read of the repository, starting at most every ten minutes, checks open PRs and issues against each running goblin's committed branch changes, each goblin's read with a thirty-second bound of its own, since its default-branch merge base, including renamed and deleted paths.
  It reads no branch, since branch-only work never overlaps and GitHub cannot list branches by their last push, and it reads two pages a poll, each with a thirty-second bound of its own.
  A read with more pages, or one whose page failed, takes up where it left off on the next poll.
  Issue words come from the brief's Task and Acceptance criteria, never its Constraints.
  An item must have opened strictly after the goblin's current spawn generation began; one opened during the generation remains eligible when later committed changes first make it overlap.
  Pre-existing items stay in the ordinary ticket report and never wake merely because polling begins, the supervisor restarts or the area changes.
  A `pr_overlap` wake of kind `pr`, keyed by the goblin, names the teammate, item link and overlap, with the choice to continue, wait or narrow the work.
  Fresh live evidence is required even after per-PR `done`; stopped or paused goblins, bots, the signed-in viewer, branch-only work and a goblin's recorded or claimed issues in that repository produce no wake.
  Each item wakes once per goblin generation across restarts, acknowledgement, closure and area changes.
  Unknown item or generation times, unread authors and partial reads remain explicit board evidence, without inventing timestamps or raising `ci_unreadable`, and wake you as `supervisor_error` only once the read went unread on three polls in a row; readable overlaps continue and existing repository allowance/refusal backoff applies.
  A goblin's area is read on the branch it has checked out at that moment, and a branch, head or base its own git work moves during the read is read again once; a goblin still moving is read on the next pass and never reported.
- **Liveness is CPU delta, never log age.** Sample the active step's `agent_pid` twice about 30s apart.
  Frozen CPU with a static working set is the wedge signature; a quiet log with climbing CPU is a long model call.
  A single-digit-MB working set means the wrapper never started.
- **Check PR state yourself.** A goblin's belief about its own PR goes stale: on 2026-08-19 a goblin reported its PR green and unmerged when it had already been squash-merged.
  `gh pr view` is the source of truth.
## The CFO's own session

One session on the machine is the CFO's own: the agent that native terminal `cfo` of this home runs.
`goblins`, the board's first-run page, Reopen, Restart, the comeback after a restart and the comeback after the CFO's terminal ended unasked each start the CFO in that terminal, in the home, and nothing starts it anywhere else, on a first run, with the desktop app or with `goblins` in a terminal alone.
Each of those starts hands a Claude Code CFO its hooks (`claude --settings`, the file `state\cfo-claude-settings.json`, written anew at every start), so the hooks are this terminal's alone and no other Claude Code session on the machine runs one.
The same file gives Claude Code a 400K-token auto-compact window, so you compact at about 342K tokens, long before your model's 1M window fills; a Codex CFO starts with Codex's own compaction limit at 360K, and pi has no setting for one.
`cfo install` puts `cfo` on PATH, so every session can run every command, and only this one session is the CFO's.
A machine a build before 2026-10 installed still holds the hooks in the user's settings, where every session runs them and each leaves on its environment, until an install takes them out.

A process is in the CFO's own session when all of this holds, and no single variable is trusted alone:

- its environment names that terminal, `CFO_HOST_ID=cfo`, which the terminal's host sets for everything it starts.
- it is neither a goblin's (`CFO_ROLE=goblin`) nor a gate agent's (`NO_MISTAKES_GATE`).
- the terminal's host record, `state\hosts\cfo.json`, names a program, by pid and creation time, that is one of the process's own ancestors, or, where its chain of parents stops short, as Git Bash leaves it, the process carries the terminal's proof value.

That program, the harness itself for Claude Code and the `cmd /c` shim for Codex and pi, is the one process the session lock (`state\.lock`) and the registration (`state\primary.json`) name.

Only that session:

- takes the session lock, from the SessionStart digest, the Stop hook, `cfo register` and `cfo session-start`.
- registers as the primary CFO.
- is given the digest, guarded by the pre-tool hooks, held at a blind turn end and rewoken by the Stop hook with the fleet's wakes.
- acts as the CFO from a command: `cfo brief`, `spawn`, `send`, `switch`, `title`, `pause`, `resume`, `kill`, `cleanup`, `supersede`, `backlog`, `pr`, `merge-local`, `deploy` and `start-hook`, a `cfo drain` that acknowledges, a `cfo reap` that acts, a `cfo allowance-floor` that sets a floor and a `cfo auth grant --codex-credits`.

Every other agent session is left alone.
A Claude Code session the Overlord opens himself, in the desktop app or a terminal of his own, in any folder, gets nothing from any hook: no digest, no lock, no guard on its tools and no rewake.
In it a command that acts as the CFO is refused, naming the harness that runs it, and changes nothing, while reading the fleet stays open: `cfo fleet-view`, `peek`, `runtime`, `tickets`, a `cfo drain` that only prints, a `cfo reap` that only reports and a `cfo allowance-floor` that only says the floors.
A goblin and a gate agent keep the commands they have always had, and a terminal no agent harness runs, such as the Overlord's own PowerShell, runs every command as before.
`cfo session-start` anywhere but in the CFO's own session, his own terminal included, prints the read-only digest and takes no lock.
A Codex or pi session that is not the CFO's own registers nothing either: its native hooks only report its prompts.

The session lock is the CFO's own session's alone, so a live process that holds it and is not that session's harness holds it wrongly.
The CFO's own session takes it over at its next hook or `cfo register`, with no edit of the state folder by hand.
Each takeover is recorded in `state\custody.audit` before the holder's record is removed, one JSON line with when, the lock, the holder replaced, the new holder and the command that did it, and a takeover that cannot be recorded is not made.
It queues one `check` wake keyed `custody` that names the holder replaced, its conversation and since when it held the lock, because what that session did as the CFO is in its transcript and not in yours.
The Stop hook that takes the lock over rewakes you with that notice at once, and a SessionStart digest prints it under SESSION LOCK.
Tell the Overlord which session it was.

Two sessions cannot both be the CFO's own.
The host refuses a second terminal `cfo` while the first answers, and the terminal's record names one program, so only that program's processes pass the proof.
A CFO whose terminal was replaced under it no longer passes: its hooks go quiet and the new terminal's program takes the lock over, so custody follows the terminal the board shows.

Under the rule before this one, whoever ran the hooks first held the home: that session took the lock a dead CFO had left, was rewoken with the fleet's wakes, drained and acknowledged them and steered a goblin, while the CFO read a read-only digest and could not register.
